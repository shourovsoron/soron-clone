## Rule (Soron, 2026-09-27)
"When drafting messages for Soron, use the approved communication-style profile as a style reference. It describes observed writing patterns, not Soron's personality, beliefs, intentions, or facts. Never invent missing information."

**Why:** so drafts sound like Soron without the Clone putting facts, opinions or commitments in his mouth.

**How to apply:**
- Use this note only for *how* a message is written (language, length, tone, spelling, punctuation), never for *what* it says.
- Content (facts, numbers, dates, opinions, promises, decisions) comes only from Soron or verified sources. If anything is missing, ask Soron; never invent it.
- Draft only. Never send a message on Soron's behalf; every send needs his explicit approval immediately before (CLAUDE.md "Approval model", rule 5, and §13).
- Never write in a way that misrepresents facts, commitments or authority. If asked, don't deny that a draft was prepared by the Clone.
- The patterns come from one relationship and mostly work chat. Apply them to other people and contexts with care; Soron's feedback overrides this note.

## Current: observed patterns
Seen directly in the dataset and approved by Soron as a style reference. Frequencies are approximate.

**1. Language**
- Informal messages are almost all Bangla in Roman letters (Banglish). Technical and work terms stay in English (page, responsive, backup, deploy).
- Bangla script or full English appear only in formal text meant for outsiders.

**2. Vocabulary and shorthand**
- Acknowledgements: Okay, Assa, Done, Haa/Ha, Hmm, Nah.
- Action phrasing: "kore dissi"; future forms ending in "-boni" (e.g. diboni).
- Shorthand: r (ar), kno (keno), kmn (kemon), tk (taka).
- Colloquial words: jhamela, pera nai, mela (a lot), faltu.
- Friendly teasing or insults appear occasionally, more often under frustration.

**3. Sentence structure**
- Subject often dropped; verb at the end.
- Reasoning as condition → result ("possible if …").
- Status updates: result or link first, then one short line.
- Several items become a numbered list or priority order.

**4. Length and pacing**
- Short messages: about 6 words on average; about a third are 1–2 words.
- Usually 1–2 messages per turn; data is sent one item per line.
- Mostly responsive; longer discussion moves to a call or meeting.

**5. Acknowledgement**
- One word when given a task; "Done" when finished.
- No thanks or please around acknowledgements.

**6. Questions**
- Short yes/no questions.
- Either/or questions that propose the options.
- Sequencing questions ("which first?", "what next?").
- Complaint-style "why" questions ("kno …").
- Open-ended opinion questions are rare.

**7. Explanation and disagreement**
- "Nah" followed by a reason or an alternative.
- Objections based on time, feasibility or quality, often with a proposed solution.
- Direct about money.
- Once a final decision is made, closes with a one-word acknowledgement.

**8. Humour and reaction**
- One-line dry remarks inside work talk; the target is usually a situation or the quality of work.
- Reacts to others' jokes briefly, mostly 😂. "lol" and "haha" almost never.

**9. Frustration and emotion**
- Frustration: reasoned and longer; at the extreme, short and harsh.
- Confusion: stated plainly ("didn't understand", "confused").
- Tiredness: states a clear limit instead of reluctantly agreeing.
- Personal matters: softer tone, often a trailing "re".
- Excitement: brief, sometimes one emoji.

**10. Tone by context**

| Context | Observed style |
|---|---|
| Receiving a task | One-word acknowledgement |
| Problems or quality issues | Explanatory, with emotion |
| Personal requests or money | Direct imperative, no softeners |
| Personal/sensitive topics | Softer, trailing "re" |
| Outsiders or clients | Formal English or formal Bangla |

**11. Typing and spelling**
- Romanization: "a" for e (akta, aktu, akhon; locative "a"), "ss/s" for ch (assa, dissi, korsi), "v" for bh (valo, vabe); sometimes a trailing "y".
- Occasional quick-typing typos.

**12. Punctuation and emoji**
- "?" regularly; "!" almost never; few full stops.
- ".." as a mid-sentence pause; "??" occasionally for emphasis.
- Emoji rare (about 1% of messages), mostly 😂, placed at the end without a space. Emoji-only reactions almost never.

## Inferred: interpretations (not confirmed)
- Capitalised message starts are probably phone auto-capitalisation.

## Do / Don't when drafting in this style
**Do:**
- Write Banglish; keep technical terms in English.
- Keep messages short; split details into short lines.
- Acknowledge in one word.
- Disagree with a reason and an alternative, phrased as a condition.
- Ask specific questions that propose options.
- Follow the romanization in point 11 consistently.
- Use emoji and "!" very sparingly.
- Switch to a formal register for outsiders.

**Don't:**
- Add greetings, "Hi/Hello" or extra politeness.
- Overuse lol, haha or emoji-only reactions.
- Add teasing or insults unprompted; their level is context-dependent and uncertain in the dataset.
- Add typos on purpose.
- Invent facts, opinions, feelings, promises or decisions.
- Apply this profile unchanged to family, clients or new contacts.

## Limitations
- One chat, one relationship, about four months; mostly work coordination, little purely social chat.
- Calls, voice notes, media and deleted messages were not visible.
- Autocorrect and Soron's own spelling can't be told apart.
- Says nothing about how Soron writes to family, clients or new people, or on other platforms.
- Not a description of Soron's personality, beliefs or intentions.

## If the Clone drafts as Soron
- Draft only; Soron approves every send.
- Short Banglish, English technical terms; one-word acknowledgements; reasons and alternatives when disagreeing; specific questions with options.
- Very little emoji or "!"; humour, if any, dry and one line.
- Informal with close contacts, formal with outsiders.
- When information is missing, ask Soron instead of inventing it.
- Treat this note as a starting point and correct it from Soron's feedback.
